Introduction

Butterflies undergo one of nature’s most remarkable transformations. Rather than hatching as miniature adults, these insects pass through four distinct stages — each with its own structure, function, and purpose — in a process called complete metamorphosis.

Stage 1: Egg — The Beginning of Life

Butterflies start life as tiny eggs. Depending on the species, eggs can be round, oval, or cylindrical and are usually laid on the leaves or stems of host plants — plants that the future caterpillar will eat once it hatches. The female butterfly strategically selects these plants to maximize the chances that her offspring will survive and grow.

Butterfly eggs vary in size and color, and they may be laid singly or in clusters. Inside each egg, an embryo develops until it is ready to hatch into a larva (caterpillar).

Stage 2: Larva (Caterpillar) — Feeding & Growth

When the egg hatches, a larva — commonly known as a caterpillar — emerges. This stage is all about eating and growing. Caterpillars have soft bodies and strong jaws designed to chew plant leaves. Because their hard outer shell (exoskeleton) doesn’t stretch, they must shed their skin repeatedly in a process called molting as they grow larger.

Some caterpillars can grow hundreds of times their original size before progressing to the next stage. The nutrients they consume now will fuel the dramatic transformation ahead.

Read more about caterpillar growth

Stage 3: Pupa (Chrysalis) — Transformation Within

Once a caterpillar has grown enough, it enters the pupal stage. For butterflies, this is called a chrysalis. The caterpillar attaches itself to a secure surface and forms a protective outer shell around its body. Inside this casing, the caterpillar reorganizes its tissues into the structures of the adult butterfly — wings, antennae, legs, eyes, and more.

This process of internal transformation is the heart of metamorphosis, and though it may look still from the outside, massive biological change is happening within.

Explore the pupal stage in depth

Stage 4: Adult — Fragile Wings, New Purpose

Once metamorphosis is complete, the adult butterfly emerges from the chrysalis. At first its wings are soft and folded; the butterfly must hang and pump fluid into the veins of its wings to expand and strengthen them. After its wings are fully dry, the butterfly takes flight.

The adult stage’s primary purpose is reproduction — adults feed on nectar, find mates, and the females lay eggs on suitable host plants, beginning the cycle again. While some butterflies live only a few weeks as adults, others — especially those that migrate — can live for several months.

What Is Metamorphosis?

Metamorphosis is the biological process by which butterflies go through these dramatic changes. Unlike insects that undergo incomplete metamorphosis (where young look like small adults), butterflies undergo complete metamorphosis — meaning each stage looks and behaves very differently.

This strategy lets caterpillars focus on feeding and growing, while adults focus on feeding on nectar and reproduction.
